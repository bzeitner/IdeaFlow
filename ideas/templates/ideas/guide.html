Getting started

Turn a promising thought into focused work

IdeaFlow is the shared record for an idea: why it matters, what happens next, what was learned, and what the work produced. Start small, keep the next action current, and let the history grow with the work.

1

Start with your account

What you see depends on the access an administrator assigned to you.

  1. Sign in with Google. IdeaFlow is invitation-only. After signing in, you can read public Ideas; private workspaces require an assigned role.
  2. Look for your workspace tabs. Current, Tracking, and Archive appear only when you can manage Ideas in that status.
  3. Check the header actions. Artifacts is available after sign-in. Feeds appears when you have at least one Idea workspace role. Admin-only controls include Users, Owners, and Manage.
  4. Ask for access when something is missing. A hidden tab or action usually means your role has not been assigned—not that the data is gone.

2

Complete your first session

A useful Idea needs a clear purpose and one executable next step.

1. Open or create an Idea

Open an existing Idea from one of your tabs. If you have Add Ideas or Admin access, use + New Idea; new Ideas begin in Current.

2. Explain the decision

Give it a specific title, a short summary of what is being explored or built, and the category that best describes the topic.

3. Set the next action

Write a concrete action someone can start without reconstructing the plan. Queue follow-ups when the order is already known.

4. Add only useful context

Add source links, notes, stage, interest, repository, parent, or visibility when they help the next person make a better decision.

Good next action: “Interview three property managers about vacancy reporting.” Too vague: “Do more research.”

3

Use status and stage correctly

Status controls where work lives. Stage describes its maturity.

Current

The deliberately short list receiving active attention now.

→
Tracking

Worth monitoring, comparing, or developing, but not necessarily active today.

→
Archive

Finished or shelved. Its research, artifacts, relationships, and history remain available.

Status is not progress. An Idea can be Current and still be at the Spark stage. Stages such as Exploring, Building, Launched, or Stalled describe maturity without changing the workspace.

Use rank and interest to express priority inside a workspace. Use a parent and child Ideas when one direction becomes substantial enough to need its own next actions, research, and outcomes.

4

Build a durable work loop

The Idea page should let a person or agent resume work without losing context.

  1. Choose the active next action. Reorder the queue as priorities change and complete the first item when it is done.
  2. Log meaningful work as research. Record the topic, focus, findings, effort, quality, model, token usage, and any questions that require human judgment.
  3. Answer open questions. Answers become durable human context for later agent runs instead of disappearing into a chat transcript.
  4. Preserve evidence as resources. Resources are source links attached to the Idea.
  5. Preserve outputs as artifacts. Reports, lists, summaries, uploaded files, and external deliverables can be browsed centrally and referenced by other Ideas owned by the same person.
  6. Keep the latest effort summary current. It should say what changed, what was decided, and what should happen next.

Human feedback is a control point. Ordinary agent work pauses after two runs without feedback. Add or replace the next action, answer open questions, or use Continue work when the direction is still correct.

Reviews do not review themselves. When the latest run is Review & synthesis, scheduled review waits for new human input or a later persona-council consensus that applies a new direction.

5

Review the portfolio

IdeaFlow is most useful when priorities and incoming evidence are reviewed regularly.

A practical weekly rhythm

  1. Keep Current limited to work that is genuinely active.
  2. Open Tracking and filter for human input, paused work, council disagreement, open questions, or missing next actions.
  3. Update rank, interest, stage, ownership, and next action where the portfolio has changed.
  4. Move dormant or completed Ideas to Archive; archiving preserves history and stops new feed ingestion and scheduled agent work for that Idea.
  5. Review Artifacts and, when available to your role, Weekly Summaries for outcomes across Ideas.

Feeds

Feeds are shared RSS/Atom sources associated with Ideas. Filter and sort incoming items by Idea or topic, read summaries, and rate interest and information value. Selecting an Idea also lets an authorized user pause or resume its future feed ingestion.

Feed history

Pausing or archiving does not remove existing articles. A shared feed continues refreshing while another active, unpaused Idea still needs it.

Tracking view

Search by text, category, stage, attention, or owner. Filters apply as soon as you change them, and search updates after a short pause. Sort by human-input priority, family, rank, interest, or update time and make quick updates in place.

Ownership

Each Idea has an owner responsible for direction. Administrators can review and reassign ownership across the portfolio.

6

Make each view feel like yours

Temporary filters answer today's question; preferences establish the way you usually work.

Filters apply automatically

Changing a dropdown, checkbox, or toggle updates the list immediately. Search waits briefly while you type. The web address records the current view, so Back, Forward, refresh, and shared links remain predictable.

Your defaults follow you

Use Preferences to choose your landing page, owner scope, sorting, spacing, creation defaults, and time zone. These choices are stored with your account.

Know which state you are seeing

A page identifies when it is using your personal default. Changing a filter creates a temporary view; it does not silently overwrite your preference.

When something seems missing

Look at the active search, owner, category, stage, or attention controls and use Clear or Reset filters. A role can also hide a workspace you do not have permission to manage.

Preference order: a filter in the current URL wins for that view; otherwise IdeaFlow uses your profile preference, then the product default.

7

Read outputs in the clearest format

IdeaFlow keeps the original file while presenting a human-friendly primary view whenever it can do so safely.

Research efforts
Open a full review to read research context as formatted Markdown. Raw view shows the complete stored source; formatting does not rewrite it.
Formatted
The default for reports and summaries. Markdown becomes readable headings, paragraphs, lists, links, callouts, and tables. Uploaded HTML remains isolated in a sandbox.
Table
The default for CSV, TSV, and flat structured lists. Search within the preview and scroll wide tables without losing the original file.
Structured
A readable indented view for nested JSON or data that would be misleading as a flat table.
Raw
The decoded source exactly as supplied, useful for logs, source material, troubleshooting, or checking a formatted interpretation.
Download original
The unchanged uploaded bytes. Formatting never rewrites or replaces the source artifact.
Summary reports
Summaries always open in a human-readable report view. Raw remains available when you need to inspect the source.

If a file is malformed, too large for a full preview, or cannot be classified confidently, IdeaFlow says so and falls back to a safe raw view. Download the original for the complete content.

8

Add advanced tools deliberately

Start with the basic work loop; enable automation only when its goal and review point are clear.

Repeat tasks
Run a measurable search or collection task on an interval, retain structured results, and review each result’s status. Repeat work can be paused independently.
Persona council
Assign decision perspectives and schedule a review after an Idea stalls. Required personas must unanimously approve a direction; disagreement returns the decision to a human.
Knowledge Graph
Explore parentage and explicit relationships among Ideas. Graph users can review suggested relationships; Graph Lab provides a deeper interactive view when enabled.
Weekly Summaries
Review portfolio-level progress, tasks, pull requests, token use, and changes over a completed reporting week.
Podcasts
Configure a repeat-enabled Idea as a show, connect supporting research Ideas, draft episodes from new research, then review, edit, approve, publish, unpublish, or delete them.
Public Ideas
A public Idea is readable by every signed-in user. Public visibility does not grant edit access, and published podcast pages may be available without sign-in when a show is publicly listed.
9

Message an administrator for help

Every signed-in user has a private, persistent conversation with the admin team.

  1. Open Help in the header. The Help tab is available to every logged-in user, even when no Idea workspace roles have been assigned.
  2. Send your question or problem. Write a message of up to 5,000 characters. It is visible only to you and administrators.
  3. Return to the same page for responses. Your complete message history and every admin reply appear together in chronological order.

Understand roles

Roles grant capabilities; ownership identifies responsibility.

RoleWhat it allows
CurrentView and manage Ideas in Current. Any Current, Tracking, or Archive role also grants access to the shared Feeds page.
TrackingView and manage Ideas in Tracking, including filters and quick updates.
ArchiveView and manage archived Ideas.
Add IdeasCreate Ideas. It does not by itself grant access to every workspace.
Knowledge GraphOpen the graph workspace and its relationship-review tools.
Weekly SummaryRead portfolio executive summaries.
PodcastConfigure shows and review or publish episodes. The appropriate Idea workspace role is also required to edit the underlying Idea.
AdminFull access, including user roles, ownership, Django administration, and configuration.

If you can read a public Idea but cannot edit it, that is expected. Ask an administrator for the corresponding Current, Tracking, or Archive role when you need to manage it.
